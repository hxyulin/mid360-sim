# Moving MID-360 ground-truth investigation (2026-10-22)

Start with independent motion capture. A trajectory produced by LiDAR odometry
can absorb the distortion we are trying to measure. Pose ground truth alone also
does not establish exact scene geometry or exact per-emission acquisition times.

## Candidates

1. **[JEPLO](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ASIG-Lab/JEPLO-Dataset)**:
   indoor MID-360 recordings with 100 Hz Qualisys motion capture. The dataset card
   specifies LiDAR-frame poses and LiDAR/IMU extrinsics. Smallest indoor bag is
   EIL_Mask2 (159,834,112 bytes). Downloaded and decoded below. Best accessible
   first diagnostic, subject to significant timestamp caveats.
2. **[TIERS multi-modal LiDAR](https://github.com/TIERS/multi_modal_lidar_dataset)**:
   indoor MID-360 plus independent MoCap; IndoorOffice1 is 4.47 GB. The
   [calibration note](https://github.com/TIERS/multi_modal_lidar_dataset/blob/main/docs/calibration.md)
   states PTP synchronization and absolute per-point UNIX nanosecond timestamps.
   Those are publisher claims, not verified bag properties in this investigation.
   GT is under `/vrpn_client_node/unitree_b1/pose`, so its rigid-body-to-LiDAR
   extrinsic must be established. The short calibration note does not supply it.
   Strong second candidate for a timing cross-check; no bag downloaded yet.
3. **[INS/MNE-SLAM](https://github.com/ins-dataset/ins/blob/main/Groundtruth.md)**:
   surveyed scene maps and continuous-time spline trajectories are useful for
   raycasting. However, the trajectory is generated by CTE-MLO localization
   against the prior map, rather than independent motion capture. Use it as
   secondary geometric validation, not independent motion truth. Its manual
   also contains conflicting MID-360/MID-70 descriptions; verify actual bag
   schemas and device identity before using it.

Our existing stationary recording and the two prior optical-transfer captures
have not supplied an independently verified moving trajectory for this test.

## Downloaded JEPLO EIL_Mask2 audit

Hugging Face revision: `3bc26fd218df48e830af916983a108792bcea45c`.
The bag SHA-256 matches its published LFS object:
`7d9fa5e8882d1bb394c83133fdadcbcb3c7a8fa76145caa2f647294c0c3ca9e2`.
Downloaded TUM trajectory SHA-256:
`3a7f5ae4db916c1003ceefbc8b2a91e568c56936708f6662095753991211155a`.

Observed by decoding the bag, not inferred from the dataset card:

- 670 `/livox/lidar` ROS2 CustomMsg clouds, 6,699,552 stored points; all four
  lane IDs are present. Cloud starts are nominally 20 Hz, with approximately
  50 ms of point timestamps per cloud. Headers equal each cloud's `timebase`.
- 6,716 `/livox/imu` messages; median header interval 4.894 ms, maximum 23.919 ms.
- LiDAR acquisition timestamps span 33.550 s. The independent GT file contains
  2,460 poses over 24.739 s, missing the first 3.951 s and last 4.860 s of LiDAR.
  There are 493 clouds fully bracketed by the GT endpoints; this does not mean
  all those clouds have adequate internal GT coverage.
- Median GT interval 9.991 ms; 11 intervals exceed 20 ms, maximum 56.276 ms.
  Reject these gaps for fine motion comparisons rather than extrapolating.
- Unsmoothed GT finite differences give median / p95 speed 0.616 / 1.142 m/s
  and angular speed 48.1 / 140.2 deg/s. The largest derivative spikes may include
  tracking noise; they must not automatically be treated as physical motion.

**Per-point timestamps require investigation before quantitative validation.**
Every cloud has backward timestamp steps. Across all clouds, 44,462 negative
steps occur exactly at 96-point boundaries, with none inside those groups.
The common within-group step is 4,947 ns. Successive 96-point group starts have
median spacing 235,582 ns, versus the nominal 480,000 ns hardware cadence;
maximum spacing is 13.379 ms. The pattern suggests packet-level timestamp
jitter/reordering or an acquisition/driver timestamp convention problem. This
is a hypothesis, not an identification of a particular driver or firmware bug.
The stored messages cannot certify original packet integrity or losses.

Preserve the original timestamps. Sorting points alone does not recover true
emission times. Replacing them by a uniform 5 us clock would assume no lost
emissions and correct ordering, neither established here. Keep any reconstructed
clock as an explicit alternate hypothesis.

A preliminary +/-100 ms IMU/GT offset sweep, using finite-difference body-frame
GT angular velocities and median bias removal, selected +1 ms but still had
0.639 rad/s vector RMS (zero offset: 0.649 rad/s). This noisy diagnostic does
not establish calibrated synchronization. It neither proves a 1 ms offset nor
justifies retiming the data.

Raw data, third-party metadata and generated reports remain ignored under
`local-assets/motion-ground-truth/`. No third-party recordings are redistributed.
The JEPLO dataset card declares GPL-3.0; the author's data stays separate from
the simulator's own dual-licensed implementation.

## Reproduce the read-only audit

Use the existing `requirements-analysis.txt` NumPy/SciPy/rosbags environment.
No ROS installation, imported dataset code or physics simulator is required.
Download the bag and `gt/EIL_Mask2.txt` from the pinned HF revision above, then:

```sh
python scripts/audit-motion-ground-truth.py /path/EIL_Mask2_0.db3 \
  /path/EIL_Mask2.txt --output local-assets/motion-ground-truth/audit.json
```

This decodes both Livox topics, audits all stored point offsets, checks GT
spacing, reports checksums and performs the diagnostic gyro offset sweep.
It assumes the supplied TUM poses describe the LiDAR frame and identity rotational
IMU-to-LiDAR extrinsics, as specified by JEPLO. It is not a generic calibration
solver. The recorded lane/offset fields are inspected without rewriting them.

## Proposed first comparison

1. Confirm timing and frame conventions using gyro/GT consistency on held-out
   intervals. Audit TIERS's recorded absolute point times before committing to
   a clock reconstruction for JEPLO. Separate tracking gaps and packet anomalies.
2. Choose a static wall and low-motion training intervals. Establish its plane
   from held-out observations or an independent survey. A plane inferred from
   these LiDAR observations is an estimated scene reference, not surveyed GT.
3. On high-motion, well-bracketed evaluation intervals, compare raw clouds,
   GT-deskewed clouds and intentionally snapshot-acquired controls. Use the same
   wall reference and one fixed world alignment; per-cloud ICP can conceal errors.
4. First replay the **recorded directions and times** through a scene proxy and
   GT motion. This isolates acquisition pose semantics from our optical fit.
   Then run the nominal pattern on the same trajectory and compare aggregate
   wall thickness/bending and the improvement from deskew. Different scan phases
   preclude a naive point-for-point comparison between real and nominal scans.
5. Report sensitivity to original versus explicitly reconstructed timestamps,
   GT interpolation interval, and sensor/IMU time offset. Pose GT sampled near
   100 Hz is not exact 5 us trajectory truth, especially under vibration.

No real-versus-simulated distortion residual or hardware-equivalence claim has
been established in this investigation. The result is an accessible independent
GT source, an audited recording, concrete timing issues, and a comparison plan
that will not mistake acquisition artifacts for a simulator defect.

The first [recorded-motion comparison](motion-comparison-results.md) now measures
pose-policy error and real floor residuals while retaining these timing limits.
